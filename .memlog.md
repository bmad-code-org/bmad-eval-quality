## The adversarial gate — seventeen ways two implementations disagree, two of them mine

Ran two adversarial lenses against revision 5 itself. Not "is this claim true" — the previous four rounds
did that — but "can two implementations obey every rule and still produce different bytes". Seventeen
times, yes. First attempt at this was one agent against all eight seams and it died of resource
exhaustion with nothing written; splitting it into a grammar lens and a measurement lens with explicit
line ranges is what got output. Worth remembering: a reviewer with too wide a brief returns nothing, not
less.

Verified every finding against the spine before touching anything, and two were worse than reported. The
AD-5 registry I added in revision 5 is cited by nothing — counted all seventeen codes across the document
and thirteen occur exactly once, inside the table, so the `Cited by` column was asserting citations the
named ADs did not contain, which is the defect AD-5 exists to prevent one level up. And AD-40 enumerates
a request-side-only condition grammar, then two paragraphs later requires that condition to name a
response channel. Both sentences mine, same revision. Under the grammar as written a probe seeding a 500
is satisfied by the observation where the system correctly returned 400, so a finding about an unrelated
defect scores `caught` — catch rate 1.00 read off proof the system was fine, which is the exact hole AD-40
was created to close, arriving through the field created to close it.

The pattern behind both: edited one paragraph without re-reading what it depends on. Same thing that
produced revision 5's AD-13 contradiction and its stale "first epic" lines. A rubric pass does not catch
it; a dependency pass would.

The one that cannot be written away is AD-16 against AD-39. A step's selection predicate *includes* its
temporal clause, and AD-16 derives the evaluator-facing phrase from the predicate. So the baseline read
and the read-back either render with the ordering visible — the leak AD-39 delegates to AD-16 to prevent —
or render identically, which is the ADR-006 collapse AD-16 exists to resolve. Reorder-invariance kills the
positional escape. Read-after-write is the only case AD-39 admits the clause for. AD-16's example phrase
uses a clause-free step, which is why nobody saw it through two rounds. Withdrawn into Owed item 2 rather
than answered, because the answer is a claim about prose an evaluator reads and this project has spent
three rounds learning those get measured.

Four decisions were the user's: response predicate reuses AD-4's vocabulary rather than a probe-side
language; cited identifiers govern the witness match with quotation as audit and disagreement invalidating;
the read-back vocabulary goes to owed; exercised means evaluator-initiated and resolves `not-applicable`,
with AD-6's waiver restriction amended to name the case. Revision 6, ADR-009.

No verdict moved — ADR-008 had already put both halves behind the calibration re-run. What moved is
whether that re-run's output would have meant anything, since the number it exists to produce was
computable as 1.00 by construction.

## Round 4 — the first verdict that says build, and the reviewer sat in the room for it

Kerem's final review. Four findings, and for the first time in four rounds the verdict is not "do not
build": build `compile` after a named minimum set of edits, and make the next unit of work the half-day
calibration spike rather than an epic.

Ran this one differently. Instead of triaging a finished report, I seeded a subagent with his review and
kept it alive across the whole pass — it wrote acceptance criteria for each finding before I touched
anything, then checked my edits against its own criteria three times. That changed the outcome twice, and
both times in the same way: my fix closed the instance he named and left the class open. First I wrote an
invariant meant to eliminate operator-specific judgement and put "where the operator's meaning depends on
evidence being present" inside it. Then, having narrowed that, I resolved `covers-by-key`'s absent case by
name and dropped the quantifiers' — so `not(for-any(absentPointer, ...))` certified against a response with
no collection field at all, which is the original F1 defect relocated one operand over. Three revisions have
now made that same mistake. Worth doing again: a reviewer who is present while you edit catches what a
reviewer reading the result cannot, because the mistake is in the editing.

The substantive change is F1. Resolution is three-valued now — `insufficient-evidence`, introduced by one
operand property rather than per operator, propagating through the connectives without being absorbed,
terminal under both polarities, landing on `abstained` and preventing PASS. That closes the class instead of
the fourth instance of it. The `count-tolerance` escape hatch is struck, because it reintroduced the
`notes: []` defect it was prescribed to avoid. Scalar `absent` deliberately stays a detected defect and
AD-26 is untouched — a missing field is one of the three measured cases and converting it to an abstention
would cost a real catch.

F3 is the one that mattered practically and it was one sentence. The gate said no epic touches `compile`
until item 1 closes; item 1 closes by implementing the generator; the generator is `seal`; AD-38 puts
`seal` in stage one, which the gate blocks. The gate forbade its own exit, and that is why four rounds
produced no Monday morning action — the only permitted activity was another review. The spike is now
exempted by name, with its acceptance criterion, half-day budget and throwaway footing, and either result
closes item 1 because a negative result is the finding.

His stopping rule, which I am not disputing: 180 → 50 → 24 → 17 → 4 findings across five passes, every one
of the form "two implementers read this and behave differently", and prose has an unbounded supply of that
form. The spine already says the answer three times and applies it to three predicates while refusing to
apply it to itself. No fifth round. Next artifact is code.

No new ADR for this pass on his explicit instruction — ADR-009 already carries the narration and another
one would be the fifth instance of the pattern he is describing. Spine goes to revision 7, frontmatter only.

## Sign-off gate, run by Kerem against revision 7

Three of four gates pass and the sign-off is withheld on Gate D, which has not been run and cannot be
run yet: the spike needs the generator, the generator is `seal`, and `src/` holds one file. Gates A, B

Gate B is the one that changed the standing of this document. The three lint rules now exist, they are
mirrored byte-identical across the three skill directories, and each is proven both ways by 45 tests
and again by mutation against the real spine. They immediately found three live defects that four
review rounds had read past: AD-3, AD-10 and AD-38 each said a `web`, `cli` or `mcp` interface fails
at compile time without naming `unsupported-interface-kind`, while a fourth site in Deferred named it
correctly. Then, once rule 2 was extended to read plain-prose enumerations, it found a fourth
instance of F2's exact signature — AD-31 reads "channel roles" as a declaration AD-19 requires and
AD-19 never enumerated them. Four rounds of careful human reading missed that; a regex found it in one
run. That is the whole argument for converting the finding classes into CI rather than reviewing for
them again.

Gate C's hand-authoring earned its half hour the same way it did in round four. A cursor-paginated,
soft-delete-filtered, 202-then-poll export API produced nine guess points and zero blocking ones, and
four of the nine were one class: AD-19 named channel roles, expected cardinality and the reference-set
declaration and gave none of them a value space. Expected cardinality is the one with teeth. For a
page of twenty drawn from ninety-seven no integer is true, so rule 6's satisfaction predicate could
not be written at all — it has to branch on whole-versus-paged, and the bijection and the injection
report opposite outcomes on one contract. All three now have value spaces and rule 6 is derivable.

The predicate count went 11 of 14 to 13 of 14. Rule 7 relevance was closed by F2. Rule 3 satisfaction
turned out to be derivable already: round four's objection was that malformed-input content lives in
the direction's negative domain, which AD-3 exempts from alignment, and that is true and beside the
point, because satisfaction never had to run through alignment — AD-39's `type-violating` matcher is a
declaration and an oracle addressing that step is a declaration.

Rule 2 satisfaction is the fourteenth and it stays open, as Owed item 4, declared non-gating by name.
It is a schema-shape decision rather than a repair: a per-operation response descriptor is exact and
invalidates the shape every existing artifact carries, while a permitted-keys denominator is free and
vacuous for any interface whose operations return disjoint bodies. Hand-authoring established that
disjoint bodies are the ordinary case rather than the exotic one — a job resource, a page of rows and
an error share no required key — and the notes toy hid it because all three of its operations returned
one envelope. That choice is a product decision and it was recorded, not taken. Declaring item 4 a
gate would have repeated precisely the defect ADR-009 had to exempt the spike from.

Gate D's instrument is verified sharp. `cc-h0-03` v1 and v2 are byte-equal outside B-002, both declare
`permittedInterfaces: ["api"]`, and the delta is entirely evidence precondition with zero new
assertion. One thing follows from that and is worth planning for instead of discovering: AD-3's
direction is closed at evidence targets, relation, polarity, scope and negative domain, and an
evidence precondition is none of them, so a generator emitting from today's field set has no field to
emit the winning delta from. The spike is still worth the half day, because a negative result closes
item 1 and because "likely" is not a measurement. But the outcome to plan for is adding Owed item 1's
evidence-precondition dimension, not finding out whether one is needed.
